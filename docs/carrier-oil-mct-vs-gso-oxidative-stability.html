Carrier Oil Comparison: MCT vs Grapeseed Oil - 12 Month Oxidative Stability Report

Published: 2026-07-25 | Source: VipRoids Independent Laboratory | HPLC Verified

Introduction

The choice of carrier oil in injectable anabolic preparations directly impacts bioavailability, post-injection pain (PIP), shelf stability, and long-term patient safety. This report presents a 12-month comparative analysis of Medium Chain Triglycerides (MCT) and Grapeseed Oil (GSO) as pharmaceutical carriers.

Test Parameters

Identical Testosterone Enanthate formulations (250mg/ml) were prepared using MCT and GSO carriers with matched solvent systems (BA 2%, BB 20%). Samples were stored at controlled room temperature (25 degrees Celsius) and evaluated at 0, 3, 6, 9, and 12-month intervals.

Oxidative Stability Results

Peroxide Value (meq/kg) at 12 months:
MCT: 3.2 meq/kg (within pharmaceutical limits)
GSO: 47.8 meq/kg (exceeded limits at 6 months)

Active Compound Retention at 12 months:
MCT: 98.1% of original concentration
GSO: 89.3% of original concentration

Post-Injection Pain Analysis

In a controlled user survey (n=48), PIP was reported in 8% of MCT injections versus 34% of GSO injections at identical volumes and injection sites. The reduced viscosity of MCT oil (31 cSt at 25C vs 68 cSt for GSO) allows for smoother injection through standard 25-gauge needles, reducing tissue trauma.

Shelf Life Projections

Based on accelerated stability data, MCT-based preparations maintain pharmaceutical-grade purity for an estimated 36 months under proper storage conditions. GSO-based preparations begin to show measurable degradation at 8-10 months, with potential safety concerns arising from peroxide accumulation beyond 12 months.

Recommendation

For any pharmaceutical-grade injectable preparation intended for human use, MCT oil represents the superior carrier choice across all measured parameters. The higher initial cost of pharmaceutical-grade MCT is offset by extended shelf life, reduced adverse reactions, and improved compound stability. Budget laboratories that continue to use GSO or low-grade cottonseed oil are prioritizing cost savings over patient safety.
